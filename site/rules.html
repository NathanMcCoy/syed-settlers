The island game

Goal

Be the first to reach 10 Renown ⚙ during your own turn.

Renown from
Each Hut1
Each Lodge2
Each Lighthouse project1
Longest Path (at least 5 Paths in a line)2
Most Pilots (at least 3 Pilots played)2

The island

A new island every game, made from a seed (the same seed always makes the same island). Nineteen hexes: 4 Forest (Timber), 3 Quarry (Stone), 4 Fields (Grain), 3 Mine (Copper), 4 Paddy (Fish, from bettas in flooded fields) and 1 Dunes (nothing). Numbers 2–12 (never 7) are laid in the classic spiral. Nine markets sit on the coast: four trade 3 of anything for 1, five trade 2 of one resource for 1.

  • No building may be next to another building: they stay at least two edges apart.
  • A Hut must touch one of your Paths (except in setup). A Path joins your buildings or Paths and can't continue past another player's building.

Setup

Each player places a Hut and a Path, then the same again in reverse order. Your second Hut collects one of each resource around it.

A turn

  1. You may use a project before rolling.
  2. Roll two dice. Every hex with that number pays 1 resource to each Hut and 2 to each Lodge around it (not the squall's hex). The bank has 19 of each; if it can't pay everyone, nobody gets that resource, unless only one player is owed it.
  3. Trade, build, buy projects and use one project, in any order. Then end your turn.

Rolling a 7

Everyone holding more than 7 cards ⚙ loses half (their choice). The roller moves the squall to any other land hex and steals one random card from a player with a building there. A hex under the squall produces nothing.

Trading

With the bank 4:1, at a 3:1 market, or 2:1 at a resource's own market (you need a building on it). With other players: the player whose turn it is offers cards for cards; the others accept, decline or make a counter-offer. An open offer goes to the first who accepts.

5 and 6 players

  • The large island: 30 hexes in rows of 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4, 3, with 2 Dunes, 11 markets and 24 of each resource in the bank. The project deck grows to 34.
  • Paired turns: after each player's turn, the player three seats on gets an extra build phase: no roll and no trading with players, but they may build, buy, use one project and trade with the bank and markets. They can win in it.

The sea expansion

Pick a sea scenario when starting a game. Ships carry your settlers across the water to other islands. Every scenario's map is new each game, made to its rules.

New Shores Playable

  • The map: the main island and small islands, with sea between every piece of land. Setup is on the main island only. Played to 14 Renown.
  • Ships (Timber + Fish, 15 each) go on edges touching the sea. A ship joins your buildings or ships (not Paths), never passes another player's building, and never touches the pirate's hex. A coastal setup Hut may take a ship instead of a Path. You may build a Hut where your ship reaches land.
  • Moving a ship: once a turn, move one of your ships with an open end (nothing of yours at one end) that you didn't build this turn.
  • Longest Path counts Paths and ships; they join only at one of your buildings.
  • Gold fields (striped) pay a resource of your choice: 1 per Hut, 2 per Lodge.
  • The pirate: after a 7 or a Pilot, move the squall or the pirate, then steal from a player with a ship on its edges.
  • Road Crew places Paths, ships, or one of each.
  • Island bonus: your first Hut on each small island earns 2 Renown tokens.

Four Islands Playable

Four islands of five hexes (six islands with 5-6 players), no main island. Set up on one or two islands: those are your home islands, and your first Hut on each other island earns 2 Renown tokens. Played to 13.

Fog Islands Playable

Two islands to start on, and two areas hidden in fog (grey). When your Path or ship touches a corner of a hidden hex, it's turned over: sea, or land with a number, and you take one of what it produces (gold: your choice). Played to 12.

Through the Desert Playable

A desert of two Dunes runs along one side of the main island, with a strip of four hexes beyond it and two small islands holding the gold. Set up on the main island; your first Hut on the strip and on each small island earns 2 Renown tokens. Played to 14.

The Forgotten Tribe Playable

Around the small islands, sea edges hold prizes: 8 Renown tokens (R), 4 face-down projects (P) and 3 markets (M). The first ship built or moved onto one takes it. A project counts as bought this turn. A market must be placed on the coast by one of your buildings, with an edge between it and any other market; you keep it until you have such a spot. Huts and the squall only next to numbered hexes. Played to 13.

Cloth Playable

Two large islands and four small ones made of eight villages, each with 5 cloth. Nobody builds on the small islands. Link one of your buildings to a village with Paths and ships: you take 1 cloth at once, and 1 more each time its number is rolled. 2 cloth = 1 Renown. Three setup Huts each (only the third collects), no Longest Path, ships on a route to a village can't move, and the pirate can't move until you're linked to a village. Played to 14, or when five villages are empty the most Renown wins (then the most cloth).

The Pirate Islands Playable

No squall. A pirate fleet (dark square) circles the main island by the lower die each roll and attacks the players with buildings on its hex: if its number beats your warships you lose a card plus one per Lodge; if your warships beat it you take a resource. Each player starts with a Hut and a ship facing their own pirate islet, sets up two more Huts, and builds one line of ships by a shortest route to their beachhead (⚑, where a Hut may go) and on to their fortress (☠ with its lairs). Pilots only turn your line's first normal ship into a warship. When your line reaches the fortress, you attack at the end of each turn: more warships than a die takes a lair (three take the fortress, which becomes your Hut); fewer lose two ships, a tie one. A fortress and 10 Renown win. No Longest Path or Most Pilots.

Wonders Playable

New Shores' kind of map with no pirate, five wall markers (W) and two bridge markers (B) on the main island's coast, and small islands worth 1 Renown each. Claim one wonder once you meet its requirement (one each, never the same): the Great Bridge (a building on a B), the Great Wall (a building on a W), the Grand Theater (2 Lodges), the Grand Monument (a Lodge on a market and a line of 5 Paths or ships) or the Grand Castle (a Lodge and 6 Renown). Then build its four levels, each costing the same five cards. Finish it to win, or reach 10 Renown with a higher level than everyone else.

New World Playable

Three to five random islands from the rulebook's tiles, ten markets. Set up anywhere; every island beyond your home islands earns 1 Renown token. Played to 12.

The variants box: variants Playable

Small changes that mix with any game: tick them under Variants when starting a game.

  • Friendly squall: the squall leaves alone any player with only 2 Renown.
  • Strongest Ports: a title worth 2 Renown for the most Renown in buildings on markets (at least 3). Play to 11.
  • Event cards: a deck of 36 numbered cards (the same spread as two dice) replaces the dice. Each card has an event, resolved before its number produces:
    • every 7 is the squall attacks (a 7 as usual);
    • a beautiful day: nothing happens;
    • the squall flees to the Dunes;
    • conflict: the holder of Most Pilots (or whoever has played the most) robs anyone; trade advantage: Longest Path robs anyone;
    • calm seas (the most buildings on markets), tournament (the most Pilots played) and a plentiful year (everyone): a resource of your choice;
    • good neighbours: everyone gives a card to the player on their left; a helpful neighbour: the leader gives a card to someone behind;
    • epidemic: Lodges produce 1, not 2;
    • earthquake: everyone damages one of their Paths. No new Paths until you repair it (Timber + Copper), and no Huts next to a damaged Path.
    A New Year card sits five cards from the bottom and reshuffles the deck. (Which card carries which event is our own mix: the rulebook shows the cards only as pictures.)
  • Two players (exactly 2 seats): two neutral colours start with a Hut each. Every Path you build makes you build a neutral Path; every Hut, a neutral Hut (or a Path if no Hut fits). A neutral can hold Longest Path. Two production rolls a turn, the second a different number. Trade tokens: 2 for a Hut by the Dunes, 1 for a Hut on the coast, 2 for cashing in a played Pilot (once a turn). Once a turn, spend 1 (2 if you're ahead) on a forced trade (take 2 random cards, give 2 of your choice back) or to send the squall to the Dunes.

The city expansion Playable

Lodges make commodities, knights defend the island from raiders, and progress cards replace projects. Tick City expansion on the New Game screen. Played to 13 Renown (with the sea: scenarios 1, 4 or 9, at their target + 2). Your improvements and progress cards are in the game's City tab; walls and knights are in the Build tab.

Setup and the turn

  • Your second setup building is a Lodge; it collects your starting resources. There are no projects and no Most Pilots (Longest Path stays).
  • Three dice: the red and yellow dice for production, and an event die rolled with them and settled first. Its three ship faces move the raiders a step closer; its three gate faces (Science, Trade, Politics) give a progress card of that colour to everyone whose level on that track is at least 1 and at least the red die minus 1 (level 1: red 1–2, level 2: 1–3, up to level 5: any).
  • The squall waits beside the island until the raiders first land; until then a 7 only makes big hands lose half. With the sea, the pirate waits too.

Commodities and improvements

  • A Lodge on a Forest makes 1 Timber + 1 Paper; on a Paddy 1 Fish + 1 Cloth; on a Quarry 1 Stone + 1 Coin. On Fields and Mines it makes 2 as usual; gold gives no commodities. Commodities count toward the hand limit, can be stolen and traded (with players, and with the bank 4:1 or 3:1 at a general market).
  • Three tracks, each paid in its commodity: Science (Paper), Trade (Cloth), Politics (Coin). Level n costs n of it, and you need a Lodge on the island. Level 3 powers: Science — a resource of your choice whenever a roll (not a 7) gives you nothing; Trade — commodities 2:1 with the bank; Politics — you may promote strong knights to mighty.
  • Metropolis (★): the first to level 4 on a track puts its metropolis on one of their Lodges (2 more Renown; it can't be pillaged). The first to level 5 takes it for good, even from a level-4 holder. Without a Lodge free for it you can't buy that level.
  • Walls (2 Copper): under a Lodge, at most 3; each raises your hand limit by 2. A pillaged Lodge loses its wall.

Knights

  • Each player has 2 basic (1), 2 strong (2) and 2 mighty (3) knights. Recruit (Fish + Stone): a basic knight, inactive, on a free corner by your Path (no spacing rule). Promote (Fish + Stone): one level up, once per knight a turn (mighty needs Politics 3). Activate (Grain).
  • A knight that was active when your turn began may move along your Paths (past your own pieces, not others'), displace a weaker knight of another player (who moves theirs along their own Paths, or takes it back if it can't), or chase the squall from a corner of its hex (you move it and steal as after a 7). Each action makes the knight inactive.
  • Knights block Hut spots, other players' Paths, and break their Longest Path, like buildings.

The raiders

  • After 7 ship faces they land. Their strength is the number of Lodges on the island; the defence is every active knight's level added up.
  • Raiders stronger: the players who gave the least defence (nothing counts as least) each choose a Lodge that becomes a Hut; if none of them has a Lodge that can be pillaged, the next lowest do. Defence as strong or stronger: the single best defender gets a Renown token; tied best each draw a progress card from a deck of their choice.
  • Then every knight becomes inactive and the raiders start again.

Progress cards

54 cards in three decks. Hold at most 4: drawn off your turn, drop down to 4 at once; on your turn, by the end of it. Play any number on your turn after rolling (Fixed Roll before rolling). Renown cards are shown as soon as they're drawn. Played and dropped cards go under their deck.

  • Science: Fixed Roll ×2 (choose the production dice, then roll the event die), Crane ×2 (next improvement 1 cheaper), Engineer (a free wall), Number Swap ×2 (swap two numbers, not 2, 6, 8 or 12), Irrigation ×2 (2 Grain per Fields hex by your buildings), Medicine ×2 (a Lodge for 2 Stone + 1 Grain), Quarrying ×2 (2 Stone per Quarry hex by your buildings), Printing Press (1 Renown), Path Builders ×2 (2 free Paths or ships), Smithing ×2 (promote up to 2 knights free).
  • Trade: Harbour Deal ×2 (offer each player with commodities a resource; they give a commodity of their choice), Guild Dues ×2 (take 2 cards of your choice from a player with more Renown), Merchant ×6 (place the merchant on a hex by your building: 1 Renown while you hold it, that hex's resource 2:1), Trade Fleet ×2 (name a card kind: 2:1 with the bank this turn), Monopoly ×4 (name a resource: everyone gives you 2, or their 1), Goods Monopoly ×2 (name a commodity: everyone gives you 1).
  • Politics: Charter (1 Renown), Diplomacy ×2 (remove an open Path — your own: build one free), Rally ×2 (activate all your knights free), Spy ×3 (see a player's progress cards and take one), Intrigue ×2 (push off a knight touching your Paths), Taxes ×2 (after the first landing: move the squall and take a card from everyone there), Treason ×2 (a player removes a knight; you may place one of yours no stronger, same status), Wedding ×2 (everyone with more Renown gives you 2 cards of their choice), Sabotage ×2 (everyone with as much Renown or more discards half).

Our simplifications: a metropolis goes on your Lodge with the best dice; Treason places your strongest knight allowed; a pillaged Lodge becomes a Hut even with no Hut left in hand. With 5–6 players: 18 of each commodity and the large island, otherwise the same.

The variants box: scenarios

Each scenario is played on its own (no sea or city expansion). Pick one under Variants box scenario on the New Game screen.

Fishing Playable

  • The Lake replaces the Dunes, in the middle of the island (the centre hex or one next to it). It pays on 2, 3, 11 and 12. Six fishing grounds, numbered 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, lie in the sea round the coast.
  • When their number is rolled, every building on their corners draws fish tokens from the bag: a Hut 1, a Lodge 2, in turn order from the roller. The bag holds 11 one-fish, 10 two-fish and 8 three-fish tokens, and the old boot. Your tokens' values are secret; everyone sees how many you have. Hold at most 7 (the boot aside). A second setup Hut by the Lake or a fishing ground draws one token.
  • Fish aren't cards: they don't count on a 7, can't be stolen and can't be traded. Spent tokens go back in the bag when it runs out.
  • On your turn, after rolling, spend fish (extra fish on the tokens you hand in are lost): 2 sends the squall off the island (it comes back with the next 7 or Pilot), 3 steals a card from a player of your choice, 4 takes a resource of your choice, 5 builds a free Path, 7 gives you a free project.
  • The old boot: whoever draws it shows it at once and needs 1 more Renown to win. On your turn you may pass it to a player with as much Renown as you, or more.
  • The squall starts off the island and enters with the first 7 or Pilot. It may sit on the Lake (blocking all four of its numbers), never on a fishing ground. 10 Renown to win.

Our simplifications: a fishing ground pays every building on its coast corners; spending hands in the tokens that waste the fewest fish; at 7 tokens you simply draw no more (the rulebook lets you swap one).

Rivers Playable

  • Two rivers (blue lines) run in from the coast, each through 3 hexes, one of them a Swamp (the Swamps take the Dunes' place: no number; the squall starts on one). The hexagon has one Paddy fewer, so 17 hexes carry numbers and the 2 shares the 12's hex (it pays on both).
  • Bridge sites (dashed) are where a river crosses an edge: at its mouth and between its hexes. No Path goes there, only a bridge (Timber + 2 Copper; 3 each). A bridge joins on like a Path, counts as one for Longest Path, and can't be built with Road Crew.
  • Coins: 1 for each Hut or Path you build on a river hex (setup too), 3 for each bridge. You can also buy a coin from the bank like a resource, at your rate for what you give. Spend 2 coins for a resource of your choice, up to twice a turn. Coins aren't cards: no hand limit, no stealing.
  • Wealthiest: the one player with the most coins gets +1 Renown (none on a tie). Poor: everyone tied for the fewest coins gets −2 Renown (it can't take you below 0). 10 Renown to win.

Ours: the rivers' layout; coins aren't traded between players. With 5–6 players the large island keeps all its numbers and has two rivers.

Merchant trains Playable

  • A watering hole (no number) takes the Dunes' place in the very middle; the squall starts off the island. Three trains may start from it, at every other corner, on the edge leading away.
  • Earn a wagon: build any Hut or Lodge during your turn (one wagon a turn, however much you build). It's placed when you end the turn, after a vote: everyone in turn, starting with you, bids Fish and Grain (each card is a vote, and is spent).
  • A player with more than half the votes places the wagon. Otherwise the player with the most votes does; if that's a tie, you do.
  • Wagons go nose to tail on land edges without a wagon: they start a new train or go on the front of one. Trains never branch, and a train ends when its front meets another wagon. There are 22.
  • A building with 2 or more wagons at its corner is worth +1 Renown. A wagon on a Path counts 1 more for its owner's Longest Path. 12 Renown to win.

Ours: no negotiating votes onto places (the rulebook's step when nobody has a majority: here the most votes decide); a bid is at most 9 of each.

Raider attack Playable

  • The Keep stands in the middle and the Dunes beside it; one Forest and one 11 are left out. The 12 coastal hexes take the raiders (☗): one starts on each coastal 2 and 12. There's no squall: a 7 means big hands lose half, then you rob anyone. Your second setup building is a Lodge.
  • Every Hut or Lodge you build brings a landing: roll three different numbers (never a 7); each puts a raider on every coastal hex with that number that has fewer than 3. Three raiders conquer a hex (greyed): it produces nothing, nothing can be built on its corners or edges, and a building touching only conquered hexes counts no Renown and can't use its market.
  • Orders (Stone + Grain + Fish, used at once) bring knights, who stand on edges (6 each): Knighthood ×14 (a knight on an edge of the Keep), Swift Knight ×4 (on any free edge), Capture ×4 (a raider becomes your prisoner), Turncoat ×4 (2 coins, and move 2 raiders from two coastal hexes to two other unconquered ones).
  • At the end of your turn move each of your knights up to 3 edges (5 for a Grain); knights on the Keep must move, and none may stop on the Keep or on another knight. Then, round the coast, every hex with raiders and more knights on its edges than raiders is freed. Its raiders become prisoners: all to a lone player there; with several, one each and any left over to the most knights (a die settles ties); too few to go round, dice decide. Each player left out gets 3 coins. Then a die picks a pair of edge directions there, and those knights go home for 3 coins each.
  • Every 2 prisoners are 1 Renown. Coins work as in Rivers (bought at your rate; 2 coins buy a resource, twice a turn). 12 Renown to win.

Ours: a number on two coastal hexes lands a raider on both; the order deck reshuffles its discards; the layout.

Traders and raiders Playable

  • Three works stand on the coast instead of a Paddy, a Fields and the Dunes: the Keep (wants Marble and Glass; supplies Tools and Sand), the Glassworks (wants Sand; supplies Glass and Tools) and the Stoneworks (wants Tools; supplies Sand and Marble). There's no 2 or 12 (rolled again), no squall and no Longest Path. Your second setup building is a Lodge; your wagon starts there, and you start with 5 coins.
  • At the end of your turn your wagon moves by its move points: each step costs 2, or 1 along your own Path, or 1 and a coin (to its owner) along another player's Path; a raider (☗) on the edge adds 2. Once a turn a Grain buys 2 more points. Reaching a corner of a works ends the move: deliver a good it wants (1 Renown and coins), then take one of its goods (one at a time).
  • Wagon levels (upgrade in the turn bar): level 0 to 4 give 4, 5, 6, 7, 7 move points and 1–5 coins a delivery. From level 1 you may try to drive off a raider next to the wagon (once each a turn): a die of 6, then 5–6, 4–6, 3–6. Upgrades cost Timber + Fish + Stone, Timber + Stone, then 2 Timber + Fish + Stone twice; the last is worth 1 Renown.
  • Three raiders stand on edges. A 7 (after discards) or a Knight project moves one to a free edge; landing on a Path robs its owner. The projects: Knight ×16, Path Builders ×3 (2 free Paths), Swift Journey ×3 (your wagon moves twice this turn), Renown ×3.
  • Coins work as in Rivers. 13 Renown to win.

Ours: a works is reached at any of its corners (no paths inside it), the raiders start on random edges, goods are drawn at random from each works' two kinds.

The exploration expansion

Explore a hidden sea with ships carrying settlers and crews, and race through missions. Played to 17 Renown.

  • A movement phase after building: each ship sails 4 edges (6 for a Fish), revealing hidden hexes it touches.
  • Harbour buildings (2 Grain + 2 Stone) launch ships and hold cargo. Settlers (Copper + Timber + Fish + Grain) found Huts from a ship; crews (Fish + Stone) work missions.
  • Coins come from exploring and from turns with no production; 2 coins buy a resource.
  • Pirate ships: each player has one; a 7 places yours and robs a ship owner there. Moving past someone's pirate costs a coin.
  • Missions: capture pirate lairs with crews, bring fish hauls and spice sacks to the council island.
